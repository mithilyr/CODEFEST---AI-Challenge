1. The problem, and why the obvious solution fails

Sub-track 1B asks for answers that no single document contains. Dev question 1b_007 — "Which accord was ultimately won by the faction of which Ederon Fellgard is a member?" — is the shape of the whole track:

  • wiki/ederon_fellgard.md records his membership and never mentions an accord.
  • The page naming that accord's victor never mentions Ederon Fellgard.

The two documents share no linking vocabulary, and hop 2 is a reverse lookup: given a faction, find the conflict it won. Similarity search has a mechanism for neither.

We did not assume this. We built the obvious solution first and measured it. A competent Voyage-embedding vector RAG baseline over the deduplicated corpus:

Checkpoint 1 — vector RAG baseline
1B answer correctness 2 / 7
Complete evidence 0 / 9

Not one question retrieved every document its answer needs. Three of seven reached neither required document (recall 0.00), so the join never got a chance to fail. Both questions scored "correct" reached one document of the two or three they needed — right answers by the wrong route.

Raising k retrieves more passages. It cannot manufacture a join between two documents that share no vocabulary. That is a structural failure, not a tuning problem, and it is why this system is graph-first.

The challenge document describes 415 documents; the shipped archive contains 340 files (341 including sample_questions.json), deduplicated to 317 canonical documents and 30,553 evidence units. We report what we measured.

2. Solution overview and architecture

One pipeline, four stages, with a rule running through all of them: each stage resolves deterministically first and escalates to a model only on an explicit, testable failure condition. Nothing escalates because it "might do better."

Ingest. SHA-256 integrity check → discover → deduplicate → assign authority → load. 317 canonical documents, 30,553 evidence units, each carrying its document id, locator and authority tier. Provenance is attached once, here, so every downstream consumer inherits it rather than reconstructing it three times and disagreeing.

Graph. Wiki infobox rows → 118 entities, 265 edges, 15 relations, 0 unmapped field names — a plain Python dict of lists.

Query — three declining tiers, each running only if the previous declined, each declining rather than guessing:

Tier Mechanism Measured
1 Four deterministic templates 7/7 dev 1B; no route shape on 36 of 49 frozen questions
2 Deterministic cue routing 40% precision, against templates' 91%
3 LLM emits a validated plan, never an answer Off by default; measured once (ADR-048): fires on 4/55, all correct

A question no tier can route ends at a named EvidenceStatus — never an empty result, never an invented answer — and the page renders the partial evidence it did establish, with citations.

Cross-collection attestation. For every pair of entities the graph connects, non-wiki passages naming both ends attach with document, locator, collection and tier: 259 of 265 edges (97.7%) — 421 codex, 115 chronicle, 26 ephemera attestations. Co-mention is not proof, and the page says "also attested in", never "confirms"; a test forbids the words confirms and proves in the output.

Deliberately not used: no graph database (265 edges is a dict), no vector database (a NumPy matmul), no agent framework. The binding reason is not performance: §4.1 requires every team member to explain, justify and modify any part on demand, and a dependency nobody on the team can defend is a liability regardless of what it saves.

3. Key technical decisions

Traversal is constrained to a named relation per hop. Measured: an unconstrained two-hop search returns 5–87 candidate paths, and the shortest path landing on the right answer is frequently wrong about why. Three dev questions ask which conflict a faction won; the shortest route runs through belligerent_in — the faction fought in it — where the truth requires victor_of. Both edges exist, both are two hops, both end on the same node. Constraining collapses 5–87 candidates to 1–14 and makes the relation sequence be the claim being audited. Fighting in a war is not winning it.

Entity resolution is exact-match only. caedmon_fellgard and ederon_fellgard are 0.839 similar, are different people, and the second is the subject of 1b_007. No fuzzy threshold may silently pick a winner; a near-miss at ≥0.90 is reported and resolved by nobody but the user.

Configuration is not code. config/relations.yaml maps 109 infobox field names onto 15 relations and declares which collections build the graph. Adding vocabulary is a YAML edit — verified live, two new field names took a test graph from 12 to 14 edges with no code change. A build matching no collection now raises an error naming what the corpus contains; it previously produced zero edges in silence.

Scoring is deterministic, not an LLM judge. Three reasons: quota (~20 requests/day/model), reproducibility (a judge that scores differently across runs turns a baseline into an estimate), and defensibility (a teammate can defend 40 lines of matching rules; nobody can defend a model's opinion). It carries a negation guard, because "there is no mention of who the victors were" contains the phrase "the victors were" and a naive substring match scores it correct.

Ground truth is validated independently. eval/completeness.py imports nothing from src/graph/: ground truth validated by the system under test is not ground truth.

The number that matters more than 7/7

The four templates match no route shape on 36 of 49 independently authored questions. On the 21 novel ones, they match one.

Those 55 questions were written by team members with no access to either router, to src/query/, or to config/relations.yaml — that last withheld specifically because it is the cue router's vocabulary source, and an author who read it would have tested the vocabulary rather than the router. The set was frozen with its sha256 recorded before either router ran.

So 7/7 measures fit to seven development questions; 36/49 measures routing capability. We do not quote the first without the second. A judge can verify the freeze date and hash independently of anything we assert.

Limitations

  • The graph reads wiki infobox pipe tables only. The other three collections are ingested and corroborate edges but build none. A wiki using YAML frontmatter or HTML tables would build zero edges.
  • Every acceptable path in the answer key is two hops. A three-hop set we wrote ourselves answers 3 of 3 — but through the cue tier, at 40% precision, and it is not two-person verified.
  • The LLM planner tier's contribution is narrow, measured once. Off by default; the one measured sweep (ADR-048) changed the outcome on 4 of 55 frozen questions — real signal that it recovers something the deterministic tiers miss, not evidence of a general improvement. Not re-run routinely: it spends daily OpenRouter quota and drifts with the provider's free lineup.
  • It does not update automatically. A document added while the server runs is invisible until a reindex — about six seconds, reported with before/after counts.
  • No authentication, audit log, persistence or observability. Single process, in-memory. Appropriate for a prototype; not for a plant-maintenance deployment.

Every evaluation number here is PROVISIONAL

eval/answer_key.json: 0 of 9 entries confirmed — 7 single_review by a single reviewer (Mithil, who also implemented the system), 2 draft. No second human reviewer was available before the deadline. Independent enumeration (eval/completeness.py) reports 0 recall risks, which is strong evidence the key is not too narrow; it is weak evidence that each answer is right. An AI re-derivation agreed with all nine and does not count — not a second human, not blind. The harness prints the warning itself on every run. Nothing has been relabelled to look better.

Approaches we tried and rejected

38 failures are logged in docs/failures.md with the measurement that killed each. Five that changed the design:

  • F-025 — a validated plan, relevant to nothing. The LLM planner returned a schema-valid, name-checked, hop-bounded plan for "What color is the sky in the Ashen Era?" by latching onto the word "Ashen", and the engine walked it into a confident, fully cited answer to a question nobody asked. Three guards followed and the tier became opt-in.
  • F-016 — trusting that a read-only corpus stayed read-only. An editor rewrote 36 files and left a phantom document that ingested as real. The SHA-256 integrity check exists because of this.
  • F-015 — a single careful reviewer. A reviewer read 1b_006, reported confidence: high, and listed two valid answers. The corpus documents fourteen — membership is recorded in two disjoint places with zero overlap, and a human reading from one direction sees one half. The mitigation is not another reviewer; it is enumeration that reads both directions by construction.
  • F-008 — structural claim extraction without stripping emphasis. The ephemera wrap competing values in **bold**. An extractor that ignores it finds zero conflicts corpus-wide and reports full confidence — silent failure.
  • ADR-013 CORRECTION — our own conclusion, overturned. We predicted the 1C conflict questions would defeat a naive baseline. They did not: the codex announces its own authority in prose, and the model follows that signal without our tier model. We kept the measurement and deleted the claim.

5. AI Usage Disclosure

Full detail in ai_usage/ai-usage-disclosure.md; complete conversation logs are exported as plain text in ai_usage/.

Tools. Claude Code as the coding assistant throughout. Gemini for OCR of the 17 scanned ephemera (ingest-time only; transcripts committed, 0 calls at query time). Voyage embeddings and Gemini for the Checkpoint 1 baseline — the comparison arm, not the answer path. OpenRouter for the opt-in planner and phrasing tiers, both off by default.

AI used to build the system is disclosed above; AI used inside the answer path is confined to ambiguous entity resolution, unmatched question shapes, and rewording evidence already retrieved. Traversal — the part that produces the answer — is plain Python.

Decisions the team made, not the assistant. Recorded across 48 ADRs, with the overturned assumption left visible each time: collection-dependent deduplication (ADR-003); exact-match-only entity resolution, refusing fuzzy matching (ADR-011); making the LLM tier opt-in after F-025; keeping the fourteen-name answer set and marking the entry unresolved after F-015; and reversing our own published conclusion in the ADR-013 correction. The deterministic-first rule and the measure-before-asserting rule were written into CLAUDE.md by the team before the assistant was pointed at the problem.
